The Problem

Every team that buys from many vendors runs on the same broken system

Procurement inside small institutions and small businesses still runs on memory. A hostel mess manager, a site engineer, a pharmacist, each one calls around from a mental list of vendors, quotes a price they half-remember, and hopes nothing has changed since the last order. Nothing is tracked. Nothing is scored. And when a vendor fails to deliver, there is no system underneath the relationship to catch it, only whoever picks up the phone next.

Mess Kitchen
A hostel mess manager calls three vendors from memory to find who has onions today, at what price, in what quantity.
Construction Site
A site engineer texts four dealers to find who still has cement in stock this week, and hopes the price hasn't moved since the last order.
Pharmacy Counter
A pharmacist rings around stockists to find the best price on a fast-moving generic, from memory, every single time.

Different goods. Same broken pattern: no data, no memory, no plan B.

The Reframe

This was never just a hostel problem.

It's a fragmented-vendor problem. Wherever there are many suppliers, moving prices, and a budget that can't be broken, the same four-step decision has to happen, every time, for every item.

01
Shortage
Backend computes what's actually short. No LLM math, ever.
02
Decide
Checks real vendor price, capacity, and reliability score.
03
Order
Places the order under a hard budget it cannot break.
04
Adapt
Reacts to a real failure or capacity limit, live, on its own.
The Framework · Fully Built

Four parts. All shipped. The same core in every vertical.

The Brain
Planning loop on Groq (openai/gpt-oss-120b). Decides what to check and when. Never does the arithmetic itself.
Complete
The Hands
Four real tools: check price, check availability, check budget, place the order. Every guardrail enforced in code, not prompted.
Complete
The Memory
Every vendor's reliability and price history, remembered and scored, 60% reliability, 40% price, so it gets sharper after every order.
Complete
The Face
One reasoning view showing exactly what it checked, decided, and did, in order. No hidden steps, no black box.
Complete
Swap "onions" for "cement bags" or "500mg tablets" and the loop does not change. Vendors, items, and a budget are the only inputs it needs, whatever the shelf.
